\section{Conclusion}

The formalization demonstrates that a classical finite-injury priority
argument can be represented directly in Lean 4.  The proof turns informal
temporal reasoning into finite executable stages, explicit state
components, preserved invariants, recorded semantic uses, bounded progress,
and machine-checked stabilization.

The central methodological lesson is that priority arguments are not
inherently beyond current interactive theorem provers, but they require
representations that expose finite information.  In this development,
finite oracle snapshots coexist with monotone positive stage sets;
successful computations carry their own uses; finite stage semantics and
infinite oracle semantics are bridged by explicit theorems; and finite
injury is reduced to a small rank argument after higher priorities become
quiet.

The final theorem establishes two computably enumerable limit sets which
are mutually nonreducible in the project's oracle-machine model.  Extending
the local reducibility relation with transitivity and connecting it to
other relative-computability semantics would make the degree-theoretic
interpretation fully internal.  Even in its present form, the artifact
provides a detailed machine-checked account of the finite-injury method and
a basis for further formalized computability theory.
