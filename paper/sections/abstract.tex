The Friedberg--Muchnik theorem constructs two computably enumerable sets
which are mutually nonreducible, giving the first solution to Post's
problem.  Its classical proof is a finite-injury priority argument: it
maintains evolving witnesses and restraints, initializes lower-priority
strategies, and proves that every strategy is eventually stable.  We
describe a Lean 4 formalization of this argument in the repository
\texttt{friedberg-muchnik-lean}.  The development does not take oracle
computation as a black-box semantic relation.  Instead, it defines an
explicit language of oracle programs and a step-indexed evaluator over
finite oracle information.  Successful bounded evaluations record both
their output and a strict upper bound on the oracle positions queried; the
central use theorem proves preservation of such computations under extra
fuel and oracle agreement below the recorded use.  On this foundation, the
project formalizes the stage construction, the priority ordering, the
attention predicate, initialization, restraints, and limit sets.  Finite
injury is proved by a rank argument after higher-priority requirements
become quiet.  A separate computability layer gives an encoded
implementation of the stage function, proves it primitive recursive using
Mathlib's ordinary computability infrastructure, and derives that the two
limits are computably enumerable.  The final Lean theorem states
\[
  \exists A B : \mathrm{Set}\ \N,\ CE(A)\wedge CE(B)\wedge
  \neg(A \leq_T B)\wedge \neg(B \leq_T A)
\]
for the project's local oracle-machine reducibility relation.
